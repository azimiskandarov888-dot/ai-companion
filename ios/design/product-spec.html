A Living Friend — Product & Screen Spec

v2 · A structural spec of every core screen, for the designer to make beautiful. This is the "what & where," not the final look. Working codename: Bob — but each user's friend gets his own name (more below), so the friend is never literally called Bob.

What this app is

A voice friend for everyone — all different kinds of people, any age. (It began as a companion for one grandfather; that story shaped its warmth, but this product is for everyone — there is no "elder screen," no elder mode, no caretaker area. One beautiful app, one kind of user: a person who wants a friend.)

  • You talk out loud; he answers in a warm, natural voice. He remembers you.
  • It is an AI app that must never feel like AI. It must feel like a real, natural human being — a person you met, not a product you use.
  • The whole app must feel natural — something with life in it: nature, landscape photographs, parchment and ink, stone and old paper. Never techy, never clinical, never "assistant app."

The soul — rules that shape every screen

  • Human, not AI. No chat bubbles, no waveforms, no mic icons, no robot glow, no "generating…" spinners. When he thinks, he simply thinks — like a person does.
  • Nature everywhere. Landscape photographs are the app's canvas (sign-in, profile, the writing scenes, and optionally the companion's home). All graded to one look — one place, one camera, one day — so the app is one world.
  • Writing is sacred. Whenever something is written — by the user or by the friend — it happens on parchment, with quill and ink, or in an old handmade book. Never a plain form, never a text field floating in space.
  • You don't design a friend — you meet one. No screen lets the user edit who their friend is (see screen 4 for the philosophy and the quote).
  • Motion is natural. Scrolls roll up and drift away; pages turn; the orb wanders, settles, rests. Gentle, spring-based, unhurried. Respect Reduce Motion.

Colors — “Sunlight through leaves” (decided; full system in palette.html)

Green is the world (grass, leaves, moss); gold is the light falling on it (sun, flowers). Mostly deep green shade, with sunlight breaking through — never 50/50.

Text colors (contrast-checked — use only these)

  • On night surfaces: primary #EFE9D8 linen · secondary #BCC3AC sage · tertiary #8A9280 lichen · accent/link #E3B75A sun.
  • On parchment surfaces: primary #2E2718 ink · secondary #574C36 soft ink · accent #7E5A14 deep gold · #8A7C60 faint ink for page numbers & flourishes only.
  • Never pure white text or pure black backgrounds; never blue, purple, or gray-blue; never iOS system red/green/blue (use clay #D2735A instead).
  • Landscapes may be dawn, day, or dusk — natural light, warm and alive; the UI chrome around them stays deep and quiet so the landscapes breathe.
  • Text over a landscape always gets a scrim — bottom-up rgba(10,14,10,.82) → transparent by ~70% height — then linen/sage on top.
  • Gold is the accent for one action per screen. If everything glows, nothing does.

The imagery — photographic, real-looking, and never AI slop

The landscapes are photographic and photorealistic. Real photographs wherever possible; AI is allowed — for building the scenes photos can't provide (the scroll on the rock), for extending frames, and for cutting the living scene into layers. The bar isn't "was a machine involved," it's "does any part of it look generated." Nothing that looks generated ships.

Method — in this order (each step is safer than the next)

  • 1 · A real photograph as the base. Licensed stock of a real place — that alone removes most slop risk.
  • 2 · AI composited onto the photo (generative fill / inpainting) to add the scroll, quill, and ink on the rock, or to extend the frame. Editing a real photo beats generating from nothing, every time.
  • 3 · Full AI generation only when 1 and 2 can't get there — and then prompted like a photographer (lens, hour, light direction, depth of field, film stock), never like a wallpaper.
  • 4 · One grade over everything. Photos and AI frames are graded together into our palette — deep green shade, warm gold light. This is what makes a mixed set feel like one world instead of a mood board.

The slop tells — reject the image if you see any of these

  • Light that isn't real: shadows pointing different ways, everything lit with no true dark, HDR flatness, a haze/bloom glow over the whole frame.
  • Detail everywhere at once with no focal point, and sharpness that never falls off with distance — no real lens does that.
  • Melted small things: quill barbs, the rim of the ink jar, rock edges, distant branches, any invented text or lettering.
  • Tiled or repeating grass, leaves, clouds, stone texture.
  • Plastic/waxy surfaces, wet-looking sheen, unnaturally clean everything.
  • Wallpaper composition: dead-centered, symmetrical, subject floating in the middle, horizon perfectly level and perfectly halfway.
  • Wrong scale — the scroll bigger than the rock, a quill the size of an arm.
  • Fantasy leakage: magic particles, glowing dust, impossible moons.

What a good one has instead

  • One sun, one shadow direction, and real darkness somewhere in the frame.
  • A focal point, with the rest falling away — depth of field a real lens would give.
  • Imperfection: dust, wear, a chipped rock, dry grass, an off-centre horizon.
  • Air: quiet, empty space where the scroll and the words will sit.
  • Restraint: a plain place at a good hour beats an epic place at every hour.

What's needed (≈5 images + 1 layered scene)

  • Sign-in hero — full-bleed portrait, the app's first breath.
  • Payment backdrop — quieter; may be a crop/variant of the hero.
  • Story scene (screen 3) — a flat rock like a table with the scroll, quill, and ink on it, and empty space around them.
  • Second scene (screen 4) — a clearly different place, same rock-table idea.
  • Profile landscape (screen 8) — a calm band across the top.
  • The living scene (screen 6) — delivered in separate layers (sky · far hills · tree · rock · foreground grass), transparent PNGs on one canvas, so the orb can walk behind the tree, sit on the rock, and the scene can drift with gentle parallax. A flat image cannot do this.
Deliver at 1290×2796 minimum (iPhone Pro Max @3x), sRGB, PNG; layers as separate transparent PNGs on the same canvas. Full method, prompt recipes, the 100%-zoom check, and licensing are in IMAGERY-BRIEF.md. Optional and lovely later: morning / day / dusk / night versions of the living scene, following his real time of day.

3 · Your story — «Tell us about you and your likings»

The signature scene: a scroll, a rock, a quill, and ink — inside a beautiful landscape.

landscape №1
(scene lives inside it)
Tell us about you and your likings
…the user writes freely about themselves, in ink…
the rock — a table under the scroll · quill & ink beside
Done → (the scroll rolls up and floats away)

Purpose

The user writes everything about themselves — who they are, what they love: sport, video games, movies, TV shows, architecture, medicine… anything. From this we learn what kind of people they'd like, so we can bring them the friend they'd probably like — or even love.

The scene

  • A beautiful landscape photograph; in it, a rock like a table, and on the rock an unrolled parchment scroll with a quill pen and ink beside it.
  • The heading on the scroll: «Tell us about you and your likings».
  • They write on the scroll itself — free writing, no forms, no dropdowns. Typing should feel like ink settling on parchment.

The transition (a signature moment — design it with care)

  • When they finish, the scroll rolls itself up and moves out of the frame — and a new scroll arrives for the next page (screen 4).

The philosophy (this is why the screen exists)

You can't tell a friend what he must love or who he must be. A friend is accepted as he is — even if he doesn't like something you like, even if he lives in a very different country. You don't choose what he likes or hates, or who he has in his life — people, pets, any of it. You only choose who you would be with — not who they would be.

So the user chooses only

  • Age · Gender · maybe where he's from. Nothing more.
  • Never the name. People don't name their friends — that would be unnatural. The app gives the friend his own name.
  • Common ground — same sport, same video game, same likings (movies, TV shows, architecture, medicine…) — comes automatically from their story on screen 3. That's why a "companion character" editing screen is not necessary: the bio already tells us who they'd love.

The quote (set it beautifully under the scroll)

You don't choose what your friend will love or hate, what he'll laugh at, or who waits for him at home. You don't choose his roads, his habits, his heart. You choose only one thing — to walk beside him. A friend isn't made to order. A friend is met. Russian: «Ты не выбираешь, что друг полюбит и чего не примет, над чем он смеётся и кто ждёт его дома. Не выбираешь его дороги, его привычки, его сердце. Ты выбираешь одно — идти рядом. Друга не делают на заказ. Друга встречают.»

Purpose

They just talk — hands-free, out loud — and he answers. This screen should be calm enough to stay open all day.

States (no words needed)

  • Resting · Listening · Thinking · Speaking — expressed by the orb's glow and motion; in the Living scene, also by behavior: walking around while speaking, settling on the rock, lying down behind the tree at rest.

On screen

  • Mode A: the orb, centered, on a quiet deep background. Almost nothing else.
  • Mode B: the landscape scene with the orb living in it.
  • Quiet, tucked-away entrances to Diary · Account · Settings (designer decides the gesture/placement — they must never clutter the presence).
No chat log on screen, no transcription bubbles, no mic button — he's a friend in the room, not an app mid-session.

7 · The Companion's Diary

Not a "memory screen" — his personal diary, where he writes about his friend.

an old handmade book — “Fyodor's diary”
…my friend told me today about the sea he grew up beside. I keep thinking how his voice changes when he talks about it — younger, somehow…
…he loves football and old films, and he argues about both, which I secretly enjoy. On Tuesday his knee was hurting. I'll ask about it, gently…
page corner → turn · pages grow over time

What it is

  • An ancient, handmade, handwritten book — think the feel of a Minecraft book-and-quill, but real: hand-bound, rough parchment pages, ink script. Nothing modern.
  • In it, the friend writes — very beautifully — the story of his friend: a biography of the user through the eyes of someone who cares. Written just for the user (and for himself).
  • The user doesn't write here — they open it and read. Pages accumulate as the friendship grows.

Under the hood (already built — important for honesty of the design)

  • The friend's real memory is a different thing: distilled notes, as short as possible, optimized to remember fast. It is never shown to users.
  • The diary is composed by AI from that hidden memory on the server (GET /api/diary) — beautifully written, and rewritten only when his memory of you has grown.

Purpose

All the practical, tunable things — standard iOS grouped list, clean and legible. No scene, no parchment: settings are allowed to be just settings.

Groups

  • Voice — the friend's voice + a "listen" button.
  • Conversation feel — the two pauses (how long he waits for you to start; how quickly he replies when you stop).
  • Notifications · Language · Privacy & data (export / delete).
  • Start over — parting with a friend is serious; confirm it like it matters.
  • About — version, credits.

For the designer — putting it together

  • Design all nine screens as one world: one artist's hand for every landscape, one parchment/ink language for everything written, one quiet chrome around it all.
  • The Companion screen (6) is the hero. The scroll scenes (3–4) are the signature onboarding; the Diary (7) is the treasure people will screenshot.
  • The orb stays a placeholder circle everywhere — the owner paints the real one separately and swaps it in.
  • Target: SwiftUI, iOS 18+, buildable — landscapes as layered images (subtle parallax welcome), native components underneath.
  • Copy in this spec is draft English; the app will be localized (Russian first). Keep the exact heading «Tell us about you and your likings» in meaning wherever the language lands.

Already real on the server (so the design is honest)

  • POST /api/companion/create — takes the user's story + age/gender/origin, and the friend walks in: his own name, character, likes, people. The user never names or edits him.
  • GET /api/diary — the friend's beautifully written diary about the user, composed by AI from his hidden distilled memory; rewritten only as the friendship grows.
  • The raw memory endpoint stays internal/dev-only — no screen shows it.
  • Not yet built server-side: accounts, billing (screens 1–2 are designed now, wired later).
