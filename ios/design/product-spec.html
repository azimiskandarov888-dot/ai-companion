The most important rule on this page

Every screen is a full-screen photograph. The photo fills the entire display — edge to edge, top to bottom, behind the status bar and under the home indicator. All text, buttons, and panels sit ON the photo.

Wrong — a picture in a box at the top with content below it on a dark background. A photo used as a "header image." Any screen where the photo stops and a flat panel starts.
Right — you are standing in a place. The place fills the screen. The words are laid over the place, made readable with a soft dark scrim or a blurred pass — never by cutting the photo off.

This holds for every single screen, including Settings and the Diary, which simply use a darker, blurred version of the scene so their content stays perfectly legible.

What this app is

A voice friend, for everyone — any age, any kind of person. You talk out loud; he answers in a warm, natural voice. He remembers you, and he keeps a diary about you.

  • It is an AI app that must never feel like AI. It must feel like a real, natural human being — a person you met, not a product you use.
  • The whole app must feel natural, with life in it: real places, parchment and ink, stone and old paper. Never techy, never clinical, never "assistant app."
  • There is one kind of user. No modes, no admin area, no caretaker screens.
It began as a companion for one 87-year-old grandfather, which is where the warmth and the patience come from. But the product is for anyone who wants a friend.

The soul — six rules that shape every screen

  • You are always somewhere. Every screen is a real place, full-bleed. The app has no "background colour" — it has weather, light, and ground.
  • Human, not AI. No chat bubbles, no waveforms, no mic icons, no robot glow, no "generating…" spinners, no percentage progress. When he thinks, he simply thinks.
  • Writing is sacred. Whenever something is written — by the user or by him — it happens on parchment or in an old handmade book. Never a plain form floating in space.
  • You don't design a friend — you meet one. Users may ask for anything, but the app quietly advises against deciding too much, and he always arrives as his own person.
  • Softness everywhere. Nothing has hard square edges. Rounded corners, translucent surfaces, faint inset dividers. This includes Settings.
  • Motion is natural. Scrolls roll up and drift away, pages turn, the orb wanders and settles, scenes breathe. Gentle, spring-based, unhurried — and it all respects Reduce Motion.

Colour — “Sunlight through leaves”

Green is the world (grass, leaves, moss); gold is the light falling on it (sun, flowers). Mostly deep green shade, with sunlight breaking through — never 50/50. Full system, with every contrast ratio measured, in palette.html.

Text — only these

  • Over a photo / on dark: primary #EFE9D8 · secondary #BCC3AC · tertiary #8A9280 · accent #E3B75A.
  • On parchment: primary #2E2718 · secondary #574C36 · accent #7E5A14 · #8A7C60 for page numbers only.
  • Never pure white text, pure black panels, blue/purple/grey-blue, or iOS system red/green/blue. Destructive is clay #D2735A (dark) / #A4402A (paper).

Making text readable on a photograph

  • Scrim, don't crop. Bottom-up gradient rgba(10,14,10,.90) → transparent by ~70 % height. Text over the dark end, never the busy middle.
  • For long content (Settings, Account): a blurred + darkened pass over the whole photo — the place still shows through, the words stay crisp.
  • Gold accents one action per screen. If everything glows, nothing does.

Vertical placement — look high, tap low

Looking and reaching are different jobs, so they get different rules.

What you LOOK at → the optical centre, ≈ 45–46 % from the top

  • The eye doesn't rest at the true middle. The optical centre of a rectangle sits slightly above it — about 46 % from the top rather than 50 %. Anything placed at exact centre reads as slightly low.
  • So the orb (screens 5–6) and the scroll (screens 3–4) are centred on 45–46 % of screen height.
  • It's a small nudge — not the top third, which is too high for a resting gaze and the worst zone for touch.

What you TAP → the lower third

  • Buttons, plan cards and sign-in controls live in the bottom third, where the thumb reaches naturally and tap accuracy is far higher.
  • Never a primary control in the top third.
The thing you rest your eyes on sits just above centre; the things you press sit low. That is the vertical rhythm of every screen in this app.

Typography

Two typefaces, two jobs

  • Interface — SF Pro (the system font): buttons, labels, settings, everything the app says. Neutral, quiet, gets out of the way.
  • Written things — a warm serif (New York, the iOS system serif): the scroll, the diary, and the quote. Everything a person wrote is set in serif; everything the app says is sans. That single distinction carries most of the app's soul.

Rules

  • Dynamic Type must work to at least XXL. Nothing may be a fixed-height box that clips when text grows — the scroll and every card expand instead.
  • Russian ships first, and Russian runs 15–20 % longer than English. Design every button and label with room to breathe; test the longest string, not the shortest.
  • Line length on the scroll and diary: aim for 38–50 characters. Wider is tiring.
  • Letter-spacing: leave it alone except for small caps labels (+0.05 em).

The components you'll draw

The photos contain only landscape — no props. Everything below is designed and rendered by the app, laid over the photo.

The scroll — screens 3 & 4

  • A parchment panel with turned wooden rollers at the left and right and small aged brass end-caps, lying flat and square to the viewer like a sheet of paper set down in front of you.
  • Soft cream #F2E8D0, fine fibres, faint age marks, a gentle curl at the edges, a soft shadow beneath so it rests on the world rather than floating.
  • Covers roughly half the screen. Holds live text; expands with Dynamic Type; scrolls internally when the writing gets long.

The diary book — screen 7

  • An old handmade, hand-bound book — rough parchment pages, visible stitching, worn board covers. Nothing modern, no skeuomorphic gloss.
  • Opens as a spread; pages turn; the text is serif, warm, and reads as handwriting without being an unreadable script font.

The rest

  • Primary button — sun gold #E3B75A, near-black label, 26 pt radius, full width in the lower third. One per screen.
  • Secondary button — outlined in leaf, transparent fill.
  • Quiet button — translucent dark panel, sage label.
  • Choice card (plans, Quiet vs Living scene) — translucent, 20 pt radius, a warm border and a small tick when selected. Selection must be obvious without colour alone.
  • Soft chip — small pill, used for the optional hints on screen 4.
  • List group — rounded 20 pt, translucent, inset faint dividers, roomy rows.
  • Orb — placeholder circle only. The owner is making the real one.
  • Status word — a single soft word under the orb; no icon, no dots, no bars.

How the screens connect

First run — once, in order

1 Sign in→ 2 Take care of him→ 3 Tell your story→ 4 Who you'd like to meet→ 5 Meet him→ 6 Companion

Every time after that

The app opens straight to 6 · Companion. Nothing else. From there, three quiet ways out, and each returns with a downward swipe:

  • 7 · His Diary · 8 · Account · 9 · Settings
  • The designer chooses the gesture and placement — a small mark, an edge swipe, a long press. It must be findable but never noisy: the companion screen has to stay empty enough to live with all day.
  • Back is always a gentle downward dismiss, never a chevron in a navigation bar.
There is no tab bar. A tab bar would turn a friend into an app.

Purpose

Welcome, and sign in. It sets the tone for the entire app in one glance.

On screen

  • The photograph fills everything — behind the status bar, under the home indicator. No frame, no card, no letterbox.
  • The upper two-thirds stay untouched sky and hill. That emptiness is the design.
  • Lower third: one warm line, then the sign-in choices, over a soft scrim.

2 · Take care of him

soft translucent cards on the photo

The problem this screen solves

"Pay us and you get a friend" is a horrible thing to say, and the wrong story anyway. Nobody buys a friendship here. The money keeps him here — the user is the one doing the caring.

Copy — plain, no metaphors

  • Headline: «Take care of him»
  • One plain sentence under it: "He's always here when you need him. This is what keeps him here."
  • Plans read as time, not products: "Month by month" / "A whole year", each showing price and period plainly.
  • The button repeats the headline: «Take care of him» — never "Subscribe", "Buy", or "Get access".

How it works, so the button is never a mystery

  • The two plan cards are the choice — tapping one selects it (a tick and a warm border). One is preselected, so nothing is ambiguous.
  • The button confirms that choice and opens Apple's own payment sheet — the standard iOS panel with the price and Face ID. It does not open a website, and we never see or store a card. Apple requires digital subscriptions to go through in-app purchase, so this is the only path.
  • After Face ID the sheet closes and the app continues to screen 3.
  • The selected price sits directly above the button, so what it will charge is always visible.
Honesty rule: however warm the wording, price, billing period, renewal and cancellation must be plainly legible here — the App Store requires it, and a friend wouldn't be cagey about money. No countdown timers, no fake discounts, no badges.

Purpose

The user writes everything about themselves — who they are, what they love: sport, games, films, architecture, medicine… anything. From this we learn what kind of people they'd like, so we can bring them the friend they'd probably like, or even love. Never framed as a form to complete: it is a page to talk on.

How it's built

  • Behind: a real photograph of a calm open place, full-bleed — nothing but landscape in it.
  • Over it, drawn: the scroll (see Components).
  • A quill and small ink bottle may sit beside it as drawn details — optional, quiet, never cluttering the writing.

Copy

  • Heading on the scroll: «Tell your story» — simple, quiet, a little wise. It matches "My story" on the Account screen, so the same scroll stays recognisably theirs.
    Alternatives: «Start with you» · «Who are you?» · «Say something true»
  • One soft line under it: "Anything you like — what you love, what you do, what you can't stop talking about. There's no wrong way to begin."
  • Free writing. No forms, no dropdowns, no fields to fill.

Where the scroll sits — and what the keyboard does

  • Resting: centred on the optical centre, ~45 % of screen height. A band of landscape above; «Done» and a little more landscape below.
  • Writing: the keyboard covers roughly the bottom 38–40 %. The whole scroll rises to sit entirely above it — roughly 12 % to 50 % of screen height, optically centred in what's left. The landscape strip at the top stays, so the place is never lost.
  • The scroll never goes under the keyboard. It is the subject of the screen; if it can't be seen, the screen has failed.
  • Long writing scrolls inside the parchment — the scroll itself doesn't grow or slide away.
  • «Done» moves to a small bar just above the keyboard while writing, and returns below the scroll when the keyboard is down.
  • The rise is a gentle spring in step with the keyboard, never a jump.

The transition — the signature moment

  • On finish, the scroll winds onto its far roller and drifts out of frame. The landscape behind it never moves. A new scroll arrives from the opposite edge for screen 4.

Meet him
free writing · the caution above it, unmissable

What this screen is, and isn't

Locking users to three fields was too controlling. They can write whatever they want — age, where he's from, or a whole paragraph about exactly the person they hope for. It's their friend and their money; we don't forbid anything.

What we do instead is advise — visibly. Above the writing sits one strong, beautifully-set line with real weight. Not fine print, not a tooltip: large enough and warm enough that they feel it before they start typing.

The caution — prominent, above the scroll

The more you decide about him now, the less of him is left to meet. Then, one size smaller and softer: "Write as much or as little as you like — nobody will stop you. But most people find the friend they love is the one they didn't design."

On the scroll

  • Heading: «Who would you like to meet?»
  • One free writing area. No required fields, no dropdowns.
  • Under it, a few optional soft chips they may tap for a nudge — his age · man or woman · where he's from — which simply insert a phrase into their own text. Never mandatory, never the only way in.
  • Leaving it blank must be allowed, and should feel like a fine choice — the button still says «Meet him».
  • No name field. He arrives with his own name; if they name him in their writing, he gently keeps his own anyway.

Layout — same as screen 3

  • Scroll on the optical centre (~45 %) at rest, rising clear of the keyboard while writing.
  • The caution stays visible while they write — it's short, and it's the point.
  • The quote is read before they begin; it may fade as the keyboard rises and return when it goes down.
  • «Meet him» moves to the bar above the keyboard while writing.

The philosophy — the heart of the product

You can't tell a friend what he must love or who he must be. A friend is accepted as he is — even if he doesn't like something you like, even if he lives in a very different country. You don't choose what he likes or hates, or who he has in his life — people, pets, any of it. You only choose who you would be with — not who they would be. Common ground still arrives automatically from their own story on screen 3.

The quote — keep it exactly, set beautifully below the scroll

You don't choose what your friend will love or hate, what he'll laugh at, or who waits for him at home. You don't choose his roads, his habits, his heart. You choose only one thing — to walk beside him. A friend isn't made to order. A friend is met. «Ты не выбираешь, что друг полюбит и чего не примет, над чем он смеётся и кто ждёт его дома. Не выбираешь его дороги, его привычки, его сердце. Ты выбираешь одно — идти рядом. Друга не делают на заказ. Друга встречают.»
However much they specify, he still arrives as his own person — his own name, his own opinions, things he honestly doesn't like. The server respects their wishes in spirit without turning him into a puppet.

Purpose

The app introduces the friend it found, built from their story and whatever they asked for. His name — chosen by the app, never by the user — is revealed here, with a few warm words about who he is.

Choose how the companion screen looks

  • Quiet — the orb close and central, the scene deeply darkened behind him, almost night. Still a photograph, just hushed.

6 · Companion — the main screen

Where the friendship lives. Both looks are the same place, differently lit.

orb
A · Quiet — orb on the optical centre
orb
he settles · drifts · rests
B · Living scene — full daylight place

Purpose

They just talk — hands-free, out loud — and he answers. Calm enough to leave open all day.

How talking works

  • He listens by default. No button to hold, no "tap to speak." The user simply talks; a natural pause ends their turn and he replies.
  • Interrupting him stops him immediately — speak over him and he yields, like a person would.
  • The only feedback is the orb: its colour, glow and motion. No waveform, no level meter, no microphone icon, no transcript on screen.
  • An optional single soft status word may appear below him — «слушаю», «думаю», «говорю» — low contrast, easy to ignore.

States

  • Resting deep moss, slow breath · Listening grass green, brightening · Thinking low gold shimmer · Speaking full sunlight, quicker pulse.
  • State must be readable without relying on colour alone — motion and size carry it too, for colour-blind users.

On screen

  • Both modes are full-bleed photographs. Mode A is the same world at night — never a flat black rectangle.
  • Mode A: the orb's centre sits on the optical centre (~45 %).
  • Mode B: he moves, so he isn't pinned — but his resting place and the centre of his wandering stay near that height, and he never settles in the bottom quarter where a thumb would cover him.
  • Quiet, tucked-away ways into Diary · Account · Settings — findable, never noisy.
  • Optional and lovely: the scene follows his real time of day.
No chat log, no transcript bubbles, no send button — he's a friend in the room, not an app mid-session.

7 · His Diary

Not a "memory screen" — his own book, in which he writes about his friend.

Fyodor's diary
…my friend told me today about the sea he grew up beside. I keep thinking how his voice changes when he talks about it — younger, somehow…
…he loves football and old films, and argues about both, which I secretly enjoy. On Tuesday his knee hurt. I'll ask, gently…
turn the page ›

What it is

  • An old handmade, handwritten book, drawn by the app and laid over the landscape photo so its pages can hold real text and turn.
  • In it, he writes — beautifully — the story of his friend: a biography of the user through the eyes of someone who cares.
  • The user doesn't write here. They open it and read. Pages accumulate as the friendship grows.

Empty state — the first days

Before he knows anything, the book opens on a single short page in his own voice: "We've only just met. I hardly know my new friend yet — but I have a good feeling. Let this book start on a blank page: everything that matters is still ahead." Never "No entries yet."

Under the hood — why this screen is honest

  • His real memory is a different thing: distilled notes, as short as possible, optimised so he can recall fast. Never shown to users.
  • The diary is written by him from that hidden memory (GET /api/diary), and rewritten only when his memory of you has actually grown — so opening the book is instant and free most of the time.

Purpose

All the practical, tunable things. It may be plain, but it is not a flat black screen: the same photograph sits behind it, heavily darkened and blurred.

Keep it soft — this screen must not feel sharp

  • Generously rounded groups (20 pt), not square panels. Every other screen is soft; Settings must be too.
  • Several small groups with breathing room between them, never one long block.
  • Translucent, not opaque. No hard edges, no full-width dividers — keep them inset and faint.
  • Roomy rows, gentle type, no heavy borders anywhere.

Groups

  • His voice — which voice, with a "listen" button.
  • How he talks — the two pauses: how long he waits for you to begin, and how quickly he answers once you stop.
  • How you see him — Quiet or Living scene.
  • Notifications · Language · Privacy & data (export / delete everything).
  • Start over — parting with a friend is serious. Confirm it like it matters: a slow, plain sheet that says what will be lost, in his name.
  • About — version, credits, photo credits.

States & edge cases

These decide whether the app feels alive or feels like software. Every one of them is answered in his voice, on the same screen — never a system alert, never a red banner, never an error code.

SituationWhat happens
No networkThe orb dims to a dull moss and stops breathing. One quiet line: "I can't hear you just now." It heals itself when the connection returns — no retry button, no dialog.
Server errorSame treatment. Never an error code, never "Something went wrong."
Microphone deniedA gentle full-screen scene explaining that he needs to be able to hear you, with one button to Settings. Warm, not scolding.
He's thinking a momentThe orb shifts to its low gold shimmer. No spinner, no dots, no "thinking…" label unless the status word is enabled.
Long silenceNothing happens. He waits. The scene keeps breathing. The app never nudges, never asks "are you still there?"
Interrupting himHe stops mid-sentence and listens. Like a person.
Subscription lapsedNot a lockout wall. The companion screen still opens; he's simply resting, and one soft line offers to bring him back — reusing screen 2's warmth, never a red "EXPIRED".
Diary still emptyHis hand-written first page (see screen 7). Never "No entries yet."
Very long storyThe text scrolls inside the parchment; the scroll never grows off-screen. No character counter unless a hard limit is truly needed.
Story left blank on 3«Done» stays disabled-looking but tapping it offers one gentle nudge — we genuinely need something to build him from.
Wishes left blank on 4Perfectly fine. Button stays active. He's built from their story alone — arguably the best case.
Reinstall / new deviceSign in, and he's still there with his diary intact. He is never re-created.

Accessibility

  • Dynamic Type to at least XXL. Every card and the scroll grow with the text; nothing clips, nothing truncates a sentence.
  • VoiceOver: the orb is one element with a spoken state — "resting", "listening", "thinking", "speaking". The scroll is a text field labelled by its heading. Decorative landscape is hidden from VoiceOver.
  • Reduce Motion: cross-fades replace movement; the orb keeps colour changes but stops breathing and wandering; parallax off.
  • Reduce Transparency: translucent panels fall back to solid #1B211A at 92 % — the photo dims further rather than disappearing.
  • Contrast: every text pairing in palette.html is measured and passes AA at body size. Over photos, the scrim is what guarantees it — check the worst-case bright photo, not the best.
  • Never colour alone: orb states also differ in motion and size; selected plan cards also show a tick.
  • Touch targets 44 × 44 pt minimum, everywhere.

What's already real on the server

  • POST /api/companion/create — the user's story plus whatever they asked for, and the friend walks in: his own name, character, likes, people. Wishes may be empty.
  • POST /api/talk — audio in, his spoken reply out. Whisper hears, Claude thinks, Fish Audio speaks.
  • GET /api/diary — his beautifully written diary about the user, composed from his hidden distilled memory and cached until that memory grows.
  • Memory, learning after every exchange, and the guardrails (never medical advice, never claims to be a machine, never claims to be flesh and blood) are built and tested.
  • Not yet built: accounts and billing. Screens 1–2 are designed now, wired later.

For the designer

The three things that matter most

  • Every screen is a full-bleed photograph with the interface laid on top. A flat background anywhere means it's wrong.
  • The Companion screen (6) is the hero. The scroll scenes (3–4) are the signature onboarding; the Diary (7) is the thing people will screenshot.
  • It must never feel like AI. Every decision should be checked against that.

Also true

  • Design all nine as one world: one grade across the photos, one parchment-and-ink language for everything written, one quiet set of controls.
  • The orb stays a placeholder circle — the owner is making the real one.
  • Target SwiftUI, iOS 18+: full-bleed photos with subtle parallax, native components and material blurs over them, all of it buildable.
  • Russian ships first. Leave room for it.
