A Living Friend — Product & Screen Spec

v3 · A structural spec of every core screen, for the designer to make beautiful. This is the "what & where," not the final look. Working codename: Bob — but each user's friend gets his own name, so the friend is never literally called Bob.

The most important rule on this page

Every screen is a full-screen photograph. The photo fills the entire display — edge to edge, top to bottom, behind the status bar and the home indicator. All text, buttons, and panels sit ON the photo.

Wrong — a picture in a box at the top, with the content below it on a dark background. A photo used as a "header image." Any screen where the photo stops and a flat panel starts.
Right — you are standing in a place. The place fills the screen. The words are laid over the place, made readable with a soft dark scrim or a blurred panel — never by cutting the photo off.

This holds for every single screen, including Settings and the Diary. Settings simply uses a darker, blurred version of the scene so the list stays perfectly legible.

What this app is

A voice friend for everyone — all different kinds of people, any age. (It began as a companion for one grandfather; that story shaped its warmth, but this product is for everyone — there is no "elder screen," no elder mode, no caretaker area. One beautiful app, one kind of user: a person who wants a friend.)

  • You talk out loud; he answers in a warm, natural voice. He remembers you.
  • It is an AI app that must never feel like AI. It must feel like a real, natural human being — a person you met, not a product you use.
  • The whole app must feel natural — something with life in it: real places, parchment and ink, stone and old paper. Never techy, never clinical.

The soul — rules that shape every screen

  • You are always somewhere. Every screen is a real place, full-bleed. The app has no "background color" — it has weather, light, and ground.
  • Human, not AI. No chat bubbles, no waveforms, no mic icons, no robot glow, no "generating…" spinners. When he thinks, he simply thinks.
  • Writing is sacred. Whenever something is written — by the user or by the friend — it happens on parchment with a quill, or in an old handmade book, resting on stone in the scene. Never a plain form floating in space.
  • You don't design a friend — you meet one. No screen lets the user edit who their friend is (screen 4 explains why).
  • Motion is natural. Scrolls roll up and drift out of frame; pages turn; the orb wanders and settles; scenes breathe with slow parallax. Respect Reduce Motion.

Colors — “Sunlight through leaves” (full system in palette.html)

Green is the world (grass, leaves, moss); gold is the light falling on it (sun, flowers). Now that photos fill every screen, the palette's job is the text, buttons, and scrims laid over them — and the single color grade applied to all photos so they look like one place.

Text colors (contrast-checked — use only these)

  • Over a photo / on dark: primary #EFE9D8 linen · secondary #BCC3AC sage · tertiary #8A9280 lichen · accent #E3B75A sun.
  • On parchment (scrolls, the book): primary #2E2718 ink · secondary #574C36 soft ink · accent #7E5A14 deep gold · #8A7C60 faint ink for page numbers only.
  • Never pure white text or pure black panels; never blue, purple, or gray-blue; never iOS system red/green/blue (use clay #D2735A).

Making text readable on a photograph

  • Scrim, don't crop. Bottom-up gradient rgba(10,14,10,.90) → transparent by ~70% height. Text over the dark end, never over the busy middle.
  • For long content (Settings, Account), use a blurred + darkened pass over the whole photo — the place still shows through, the words stay crisp.
  • Shoot/choose photos with a quiet area where the text will live. That is cheaper than fighting the image later.
  • Gold accents one action per screen. If everything glows, nothing does.

The imagery — photographic, real-looking, never AI slop

The images are photographic and photorealistic, and the owner supplies them before the design work: real photographs as the base, with AI used to add what photography can't hand us (the scroll, quill, and ink on the rock) and to cut the living scene into layers. The bar is not "was AI involved" — it is "does any part of it look generated."

Every photo must be

  • Vertical and full-bleed — 1290 × 2796 px minimum, composed for a whole phone screen, not a strip.
  • Quiet where the words go — usually the lower third, and near the top for a title. Busy corner-to-corner images are unusable.
  • One sun, one shadow direction, with real darkness somewhere in frame.
  • Graded to match the others — same grade, grain, and black point across all six, so the app is one place on one day.
Method, the slop-tell rejection list, the 100 %-zoom check, and licensing live in IMAGERY-BRIEF.md. The copy-paste prompts live in IMAGE-PROMPTS.md.

3 · Your story — «Tell us about you and your likings»

The signature scene: you are sitting at a rock in the open, writing on a scroll.

landscape stays visible above ↑
Tell us about you and your likings
I grew up by the sea, and I still miss the smell of it. I love football, old films, and arguing about both…
the rock the scroll lies on
Done
quill + ink rest on the rock, in the photo

Purpose

The user writes everything about themselves — who they are, what they love: sport, video games, movies, TV shows, architecture, medicine… anything. From this we learn what kind of people they'd like, so we can bring them the friend they'd probably like — or even love.

The scene (this is a photograph, supplied)

  • Camera at a low, seated angle — you're at the rock, not hovering above it. The landscape is visible beyond: grass, trees, hills, sky.
  • A flat rock as the table (never wood). On it: an unrolled parchment scroll, a real feather quill, and a small glass ink bottle.
  • The scroll in the photo is where the interface's writing surface sits — the live scroll is laid over it in the app, matched to its perspective and light.

Interface

  • Heading on the scroll: «Tell us about you and your likings».
  • Free writing — no forms, no dropdowns. Typing should feel like ink settling.
  • The keyboard covers the lower scene; the scroll rises above it.

The transition (a signature moment)

  • On finish, the scroll rolls itself up and drifts out of frame — the landscape behind it never moves — and a new scroll arrives for screen 4.

The philosophy (why this screen exists)

You can't tell a friend what he must love or who he must be. A friend is accepted as he is — even if he doesn't like something you like, even if he lives in a very different country. You don't choose what he likes or hates, or who he has in his life — people, pets, any of it. You only choose who you would be with — not who they would be.

So the user chooses only

  • Age · Gender · maybe where he's from. Nothing more.
  • Never the name. People don't name their friends — the app gives him his own.
  • Common ground — same sport, same games, same films, architecture, medicine — comes automatically from their story on screen 3. That's why a "companion character" editing screen is not necessary.

The quote (set beautifully below the scroll, over the photo)

You don't choose what your friend will love or hate, what he'll laugh at, or who waits for him at home. You don't choose his roads, his habits, his heart. You choose only one thing — to walk beside him. A friend isn't made to order. A friend is met. Russian: «Ты не выбираешь, что друг полюбит и чего не примет, над чем он смеётся и кто ждёт его дома. Не выбираешь его дороги, его привычки, его сердце. Ты выбираешь одно — идти рядом. Друга не делают на заказ. Друга встречают.»

Purpose

They just talk — hands-free, out loud — and he answers. Calm enough to leave open all day.

States (no words needed)

  • Resting · Listening · Thinking · Speaking — shown by the orb's glow and motion; in the Living scene also by behavior: drifting while speaking, settling on the rock, resting behind the tree.

On screen

  • Both modes are full-bleed photographs. Mode A is the same world at night; it is never a flat black rectangle.
  • Quiet, tucked-away ways into Diary · Account · Settings — the designer picks the gesture and placement; they must never clutter the presence.
  • Optional and lovely: the scene follows his real time of day.
No chat log, no transcript bubbles, no mic button — he's a friend in the room, not an app mid-session.

7 · The Companion's Diary

Not a "memory screen" — his own book, lying open on the rock, out in the place.

…he loves football and old films, and argues about both, which I secretly enjoy. On Tuesday his knee hurt. I'll ask, gently…

What it is

  • An old handmade, handwritten book — hand-bound, rough parchment, ink script. Nothing modern. It lies open on the rock in the photograph, not on a flat screen.
  • In it, the friend writes — very beautifully — the story of his friend: a biography of the user through the eyes of someone who cares.
  • The user doesn't write here — they open it and read. Pages accumulate as the friendship grows.

Under the hood (already built)

  • His real memory is a different thing: distilled notes, as short as possible, optimized to remember fast. Never shown to users.
  • The diary is composed by AI from that hidden memory on the server (GET /api/diary), rewritten only when his memory of you has grown.

Purpose

All the practical, tunable things — a clean, legible grouped list. It may be plain, but it is not a flat black screen: the same photograph sits behind it, heavily darkened and blurred.

Groups

  • Voice — his voice + a "listen" button.
  • Conversation feel — the two pauses (how long he waits for you to start; how quickly he replies once you stop).
  • Notifications · Language · Privacy & data (export / delete).
  • Start over — parting with a friend is serious; confirm like it matters.
  • About — version, credits.

For the designer — putting it together

  • Every screen is a full-bleed photograph with the interface laid on top. If a screen has a flat background anywhere, it's wrong.
  • Design all nine screens as one world: one grade across the photos, one parchment/ink language for everything written, one quiet set of controls.
  • The Companion screen (6) is the hero. The scroll scenes (3–4) are the signature onboarding; the Diary (7) is the treasure people screenshot.
  • The orb stays a placeholder circle — the owner is making the real one.
  • Target SwiftUI, iOS 18+: photos as layered images with subtle parallax, native components over them, blur via .ultraThinMaterial-style passes.
  • Copy here is draft English; the app ships Russian first.

Already real on the server (so the design is honest)

  • POST /api/companion/create — the user's story + age/gender/origin, and the friend walks in: his own name, character, likes, people.
  • GET /api/diary — the friend's beautifully written diary about the user, composed by AI from his hidden distilled memory.
  • The raw memory endpoint stays internal/dev-only — no screen shows it.
  • Not yet built server-side: accounts and billing (screens 1–2 designed now, wired later).
