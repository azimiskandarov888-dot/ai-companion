# The photos — what to find, and where

**No AI. Real photographs only.** Six beautiful landscapes from a free stock site, used
full-screen behind the interface. Later, replace any of them with your own photography —
nothing in the app has to change, the files just get swapped.

**Where:** [pexels.com](https://pexels.com) and [unsplash.com](https://unsplash.com) —
free, commercial use allowed. Always download the **largest size offered**.

---

## What every photo needs

- **Vertical**, and at least **1290 × 2796 px** (a whole phone screen). Landscape-format
  shots crop badly — filter for portrait/vertical.
- **A quiet area** where text or a panel will sit. This is the one rule people forget: a
  photo that's beautiful corner-to-corner is unusable, because the words land on top of it.
- **Calm and ordinary** beats epic. A plain hillside at a good hour reads as *real*; a
  dramatic mountain reads as a wallpaper.
- **No people, no buildings, no roads, no signs.** Nothing to identify a place or a person.
- **Soft, natural light.** Morning, overcast, or late afternoon. Avoid harsh midday sun,
  heavy HDR edits, and anything oversaturated.
- Colours that suit the palette: **green, moss, dry gold grass, warm shadow.** Avoid strong
  blues, purples, autumn orange, and snow.

## The six

### 1 · Sign in — the app's first breath
The most important one. Quiet, open, hopeful.
**Needs:** empty sky in the upper half, calm ground in the lower third for the buttons.
**Search:** `green hills morning`, `meadow golden hour`, `rolling hills mist`,
`foggy field sunrise`

### 2 · Payment
Same world, later in the day. Quieter than the hero.
**Needs:** mostly shadow with one soft band of light; a large calm lower half.
**Search:** `valley dusk`, `evening field`, `hills sunset calm`, `twilight meadow`

### 3 · Your story — the writing screen
The parchment scroll is **drawn by the app** and laid over this photo, so the photo just
has to be a beautiful, calm place.
**Needs:** an **uncluttered middle and lower half** — the scroll covers roughly half the
screen and must not fight a busy background. Some sky or distance visible at the top.
**Search:** `open green field sky`, `simple meadow`, `grass field minimal`, `misty meadow`

### 4 · Who you'd be with — the second writing screen
A clearly **different place** from #3: cooler, shadier, quieter.
**Needs:** same calm middle and lower half.
**Search:** `birch forest`, `woodland light`, `forest clearing mist`, `green forest path`

### 5 · Account / Profile
This one gets blurred and darkened in the app, so **simple is better than interesting**.
**Needs:** even, uncluttered, no strong focal point.
**Search:** `treeline field`, `quiet countryside`, `open meadow sky`, `calm lake shore`

### 6 · The living scene — where he lives
The orb sits, rests, and drifts in this one, so its shapes matter.
**Needs:** **a tree** (something for him to rest behind), **open ground in the middle**
(room to move), and ideally a **rock or low stone**. Nothing cluttered in the centre.
**Search:** `lone tree meadow`, `single tree field`, `oak tree hillside`,
`tree in clearing`

*Optional and lovely later:* a **night or dusk version of a similar place**, for the quiet
mode of the companion screen.

## After you've chosen them

**1 · Check each one.** Shrink it to phone size: does it read in one glance, with room for
words? Then look at full size for anything distracting — a fence post, a rooftop, a person
in the distance.

**2 · Grade all six together.** Same treatment on every one: push shadows slightly green,
warm the highlights a little, match the brightness and contrast, add the same light grain.
Lightroom, Photoshop, or one preset in any editor is enough.

**This step matters more than which photos you pick.** Six good photos with different
colour casts look like a mood board. The same six graded together look like one place on
one day — which is the whole feeling of the app.

**3 · Export** at 1290 × 2796 or larger, sRGB, PNG or high-quality JPEG.

**4 · Keep a `credits.txt`** beside the files: photo name, photographer, source URL, and
licence. You'll want it once the app is on sale.

---

## Licence check (quick, but do it)

Pexels and Unsplash both allow commercial use and modification with no attribution
required — but check the individual photo page anyway, and avoid any shot with a
recognisable person, a private house, or a trademarked landmark in it.

## Later — when you shoot your own

Everything above is the brief for your own camera too. The short version:

- Shoot **vertical**, in **morning or late-afternoon** light, or under soft overcast.
- Leave **empty space** in the frame where the words will go — resist filling it.
- Keep it **plain**. The place doesn't need to be spectacular; it needs to be calm.
- For the **living scene**, find a tree with open ground beside it and shoot it from a low
  angle so the ground reads as a place he could be standing in.
- If you ever want the writing scene as a real photograph: a **flat rock**, a real scroll on
  it, shot from **seated height with a wide lens about half a metre away**, so the scroll
  fills the lower half of the frame. That is a real photo you can take in an afternoon —
  and it's the one thing no stock library or AI could give us.
